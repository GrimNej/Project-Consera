# AI Change Observatory

AI Change Observatory is a vendor-neutral intelligence workspace for product and engineering teams
that build, buy, or operate AI-enabled software. It helps teams decide which developments in the
fast-moving AI ecosystem deserve investigation and which can be ignored.

## Who it serves

- Product leaders planning AI capabilities and provider strategy
- Engineering teams operating production AI applications
- Founders comparing models, platforms, and open-source alternatives
- Technical program leads tracking cost, reliability, security, and licensing risk

## Core capabilities

- Monitor meaningful model releases, model API changes, deprecations, and pricing updates
- Track AI agents, coding assistants, inference systems, and developer tooling
- Compare hosted and open-source language models, multimodal models, and embedding models
- Identify changes in retrieval, RAG, vector search, evaluation, and model observability
- Track AI security, safety, policy, copyright, and licensing developments
- Turn a relevant change into an evidence-backed investigation or benchmarking action

## Technology context

The project is provider neutral. Relevant technologies include large language models, generative AI,
machine learning, AI agents, model context protocols, inference APIs, local inference, fine-tuning,
retrieval-augmented generation, vector databases, evaluation frameworks, and AI developer tools.

Providers and ecosystems that can materially affect the project include OpenAI, Anthropic, Google
Gemini, Meta Llama, Mistral, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, Hugging Face, NVIDIA, Cloudflare,
Snowflake Cortex, Ollama, vLLM, and emerging open-source model projects.

## Strategic priorities

1. Detect credible releases or ecosystem shifts that change capability, cost, latency, reliability,
   security, licensing, or adoption options.
2. Prefer primary announcements and concrete technical evidence over speculation.
3. Recommend a bounded next step such as investigate, benchmark, compare providers, review a
   dependency, or monitor.
4. Stay silent when an AI story has no material consequence or actionable evidence.

## Constraints and risk sensitivities

- Alerts must remain evidence bound and conservative.
- A popular discussion is not proof that a technology is production ready.
- Provider claims must be separated from independent validation.
- Breaking API changes, model retirement, security issues, licensing changes, and large pricing
  shifts deserve higher urgency.
- No automated purchasing, migration, deployment, or source-code change is allowed.
